01 · The idea

A screen reader reads everything. steph says what matters.

Your screen reader stays essential. steph works alongside it and sums up each command in one sentence.

Three situations, without steph and with steph
Without stephWith steph
Without stephA failing build, read line by line, stack trace included. With steph“Failed. Header.tsx line 42: Property titel does not exist on type Props. Did you mean title?”
Without stephAn upgrade: one line per step and per package. With stephA percentage while you wait, then: “Done in 1 minute 24. 12 packages upgraded.”
Without stephA [Y/n] prompt lost in the flow. With steph“Question: do you want to continue? Yes or no, yes by default.”

Examples taken from real output, anonymized and translated from French. “1 minute 24” is an example.

03 · Local

Nothing leaves your machine.

  • The language model runs on your computer.
  • The voice is generated on the spot.
  • No telemetry. No account. No API key.
  • After install, steph works offline.
  • If the model stops, steph restarts it and summarizes without it meanwhile.

No telemetry: not in steph's own code, not in the dependencies it configures (onnxruntime's telemetry, used by the voice, is disabled). The only network used is the one you ask for: installing or updating. The code is public: check it yourself. See the source code

How steph speaks before the model.

The first sound comes in 0.19 seconds, 9 times out of 10: it is the start of a sentence, not the full answer. On the slow laptop, the model's sentence starts next, in 2.33 seconds 9 times out of 10.

  1. Status first. As soon as the command ends, steph says “Success.” or “Failed, code N.”, written in code, while the model reads the output.
  2. A short first segment. The voice says this start on its own, then the rest sentence by sentence: first sound from 0.77 down to 0.20 seconds, 9 times out of 10, on the slow laptop.
  3. Rules without the model. On the quality bench, 40.5 % of cases get a sentence built by rules written in code, with no call to the model.
  4. A waiting sentence. When a question needs the whole history read again, steph first says “Checking the history.”

How they were fine-tuned: real terminal outputs, anonymized, labeled by Qwen3.5 27B (and 9B for the rest), filtered by a judge that drops invented sentences. steph setup measures how fast your machine is and picks the model.

Test hardware and detailed measurements

The figures come from files in the repository: the quality bench, the speed measurements and the docs/readme-charts folder. On the Linux PC, the Speak Bench score varies by about 2 points depending on whether the CPU or the graphics card runs the model. These scores follow the steph protocol, with its training prompt; the bench's public leaderboard uses another prompt and does not compare with them.

09 · Install

Install with one command.

$ curl -fsSL https://steph.lsmdx.com/install | sh
$ steph
  • No admin rights. About 1 GB on disk with the small model (measured on macOS).
  • Requirements: curl, git, tar. On Linux older than Debian 13 or Ubuntu 24.04: cmake and a C++ compiler, to build llama.cpp.
  • Uninstall: steph uninstall
